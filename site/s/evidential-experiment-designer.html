Evidential Experiment Designer

Use when a team has a specific change worth testing — a message variant, an onboarding tweak, a nudge cadence — and intends to run it as a rigorous rapid experiment in Evidential, the open-source experiment engine for nonprofits, but has not yet made the design decisions the tool will demand: experiment type, arms, randomization unit, metrics, power. Invoke it when a weak-link hypothesis or experiment idea is about to be "just shipped and eyeballed" instead of randomized. Do not invoke for a full-scale RCT with enumerator-managed arms — Evidential's own docs rule that out; scope it with evaluation-design-scoper and an independent evaluator — and do not invoke to generate the hypothesis itself; that is funnel-weak-link-diagnoser's job.

Evidential was built for social-sector deployments — its early partners run programs in India and across Africa — so the tooling assumptions are already non-WEIRD-aware (WhatsApp delivery integration, preassigned designs for offline-ish populations). What it does assume is data infrastructure: a BigQuery/Postgres/Redshift warehouse with a clean participant table, and client-side capacity to deliver each arm's treatment. Where those are missing, the design brief this skill produces is a plan for later, not a launch config.

Inputs

  • experiment_intent_statement
    The team's plain-language statement of what they want to test: the change or variant, who it applies to, what they expect it to move, and the decision the result will inform.
  • funnel_weak_link_hypotheses (optional)
    Optional diagnosis from funnel-weak-link-diagnoser. When present, the experiment must test a named hypothesis from it — the primary metric is then the funnel transition that hypothesis predicts will move, not a metric chosen fresh.
  • evaluation_design_brief (optional)
    Optional measurement plan from evaluation-design-scoper. When present, carry its locked primary outcome and minimum effect worth detecting into the metric section instead of inventing new ones — a rapid experiment that quietly redefines the program's locked outcome is the drift that skill exists to prevent.

Outputs

  • experiment_configuration_brief
    A launch-ready experiment configuration: hypothesis, Evidential experiment type, randomization unit and arms, primary and guardrail metrics with detectable effect, power inputs, stopping rule, delivery integration plan, and analysis plan — plus the decision rule the result feeds.

What it does

Turns one testable hypothesis into a launch-ready Evidential experiment configuration — experiment type, arms, randomization unit, eligibility filters, metrics with a detectable effect, power inputs, stopping rule, delivery integration — specified concretely enough to enter into the tool without further design decisions.

When to invoke it

  • A change is about to be shipped to everyone at once "to see if it helps" when the population, delivery channel, and data warehouse would support randomizing it.
  • funnel-weak-link-diagnoser has produced a surviving hypothesis whose cheapest honest test is an experiment.
  • ai-eval-level-matcher has recommended Level 2 or Level 3 evaluation and the team's experimentation stack is Evidential.
  • A team is staring at Evidential's create-experiment form unsure what half the fields (strata, MDE, balance threshold) should contain — the decisions belong upstream of the form, which is where this skill sits.
  • Do not invoke for a full-scale impact RCT. Evidential's docs are explicit: it is "not built for full-scale Randomized Control Trials" with enumerator-managed arms and full counterfactuals — that is Level 4 territory for evaluation-design-scoper plus an independent evaluator.
  • Do not invoke to find something to test. An experiment without a mechanism hypothesis produces a significant-or-not answer to a question nobody asked; run the diagnosis first.

What it draws on

Evidential (IDinsight & The Agency Fund, 2025) — the experiment engine named by the AI Evaluation Playbook's Motion 04 as the tooling that "help[s] teams automate randomization, track real-time results, and reduce analysis bottlenecks." The design brief mirrors the tool's actual design spec, so every section lands on a real field rather than a vague intention:

  • Experiment type is Evidential's first fork: freq_preassigned (participants sampled from the warehouse and assigned up front, with stratified randomization and a pre-launch balance check), freq_online (arms assigned in real time as new participants appear — no stratification, no balance check), and the adaptive family (mab_online, mab_online_dwh, cmab_online — Thompson-sampling bandits that optimize allocation rather than estimate effects precisely). The skill's rule of thumb, from the tool's own structure: known finite population → preassigned; open enrollment → online; "find the best arm cheaply, precise effect size not needed" → bandit.
  • Arms (2–20, optional weights summing to 100), the first arm being control; eligibility filters (includes/excludes/between on flagged participant fields); randomization unit — individual (primary_key) or cluster (cluster_key + desired cluster count, for treatments that spill over within groups); strata for preassigned designs.
  • Metrics: each a flagged numeric/boolean warehouse field with either a relative metric_pct_change or absolute metric_target to detect; Evidential defaults power to 0.8, alpha to 0.05, and — if the team declines to choose — target effect to baseline × 0.1. This skill treats that last default as a red flag, not a convenience: the detectable effect must come from the program's decision threshold (the evaluation_design_brief's minimum effect worth detecting, when one exists), echoing the same discipline evaluation-design-scoper enforces.
  • Analysis is intent-to-treat OLS with strata and pre-treatment outcome values as controls; delivery is the client's job — Evidential has no feature flags, it returns assignments (CSV, REST, or webhook) and the program must map each arm_name to an actual treatment path (e.g. a WhatsApp journey). An experiment with no delivery mapping randomizes nobody into anything.
  • Close-out: Evidential records a decision and an impact rating per experiment, doubling as the org's experiment registry — so the brief must state the decision rule before launch.

Judgment call the sources don't fully specify: guardrail metrics. Evidential permits up to 150 metrics; this skill mandates exactly one primary metric plus a small named set of guardrails (metrics that must not degrade), because a many-primary experiment re-imports the which-number-moved problem that pre-specification exists to kill.

Worked example

Inputs (condensed): MaizeMate's weak-link diagnosis (see funnel-weak-link-diagnoser) — log analysis supported H1: onboarded farmers type nothing; they don't know what to ask. Intent: test a first-question example menu against the open-ended prompt. (Illustrative — continues the catalogue's running example; not a real program.)

# Experiment Configuration Brief

**Hypothesis under test:** H1 — onboarded farmers don't engage because
the open "ask me anything" prompt gives no model of a good first
question; a localized example-question menu will raise
onboarding→engagement.
**Decision rule:** ≥8pp lift in week-1 engagement → menu becomes default
for all new farmers; null → H1 is weakened, escalate H3 (data cost) to a
delivery-model review; negative → keep open prompt, log in registry.

Design

Field Value Why
Experiment type freq_online open enrollment — new farmers appear daily; no fixed roster to preassign
Randomization unit individual (farmer_wa_id) menu is per-chat; no within-coop spillover expected for a UI prompt
Arms control: open prompt / treatment: 4-item example menu (county-localized) 50/50
Eligibility filters onboarding_complete includes true; enrollment_date between 2026-08-17 and 2026-10-12 new onboarders only — existing users have already seen the open prompt
Strata n/a (online experiments assign without stratification)
Start / end 2026-08-17 → 2026-10-12

Metrics

Primary: engaged_week1 (boolean: ≥1 substantive question within 7 days of onboarding) — detectable effect: +8pp on a 31% baseline, from the decision rule above, not the default baseline×0.1 (which would be a meaninglessly small 3.1pp for this decision). Guardrails: setup_completion_rate (must not fall — a menu shown mid-onboarding could add friction); guardrail_refusal_rate (must not rise — menu items must not bait out-of-scope queries).

Power & stopping

Power 0.8, alpha 0.05; power check against the participants table says ~1,100 farmers needed, ~8 weeks of enrollment at current recruitment rates — hence the end date. Stopping: end_date; no early stopping on interim p-values (online design has no balance check, so mid-stream peeks are doubly untrustworthy at small n).

Delivery integration

Product lead maps arm_name → WhatsApp flow: assignment fetched per farmer at onboarding-complete via the per-participant assignment endpoint; "menu" routes to the menu journey, "control" to the current prompt. Webhook on commit notifies the backend to activate the mapping.

Analysis plan

ITT on engaged_week1 via Evidential's OLS; no strata controls (online); research lead reads the dashboard at end date, records decision + impact in the registry. Small-sample caution: check arm sizes for drift weekly since online assignment carries imbalance risk.

Data & ethics notes

Participants table: farmers view, unique id farmer_wa_id (hashed — raw phone numbers must not be the id). Message variants fall under the program's existing service-improvement consent; no additional data collected beyond standard logs. ```

Known failure modes

  • Shipping the tool's default effect size. Accepting baseline × 0.1 because the field auto-filled. A detectable effect that no decision hangs on produces a study that is powered, significant, and useless — the same failure evaluation-design-scoper polices, re-imported through a form field.
  • Bandit envy. Choosing a bandit because "adaptive sounds efficient" when the team needs a defensible effect estimate for a scale decision. Bandits allocate toward winners; they answer "which arm" cheaply, not "how much better, with what confidence."
  • The unmapped arm. A committed experiment whose treatment arm no system actually delivers — Evidential returns assignments, nothing more, and a missing arm→journey mapping means both arms silently get control. The delivery section's "if empty, the experiment does not exist" line is load-bearing.
  • Individual randomization under spillover. Randomizing farmers within cooperatives when the treatment travels by word of mouth — contamination shrinks the measured effect toward null. If the treatment is discussable, the unit is the cluster.
  • Metric sprawl. Fifteen "primary-ish" metrics, one of which will clear p<0.05 by luck. One primary, named guardrails, everything else exploratory-and-labeled — or the registry entry is HARKing bait.
  • Peeking at online experiments. Stopping a freq_online experiment the first week the dashboard shows a star — with no balance check and small interim n, that star is noise more often than signal.
  • Out of scope: no warehouse, no delivery capacity, or Level 4 questions. Without a queryable participant table (or for pure in-person delivery), and for cost-effectiveness-of-the-program questions, this brief can only document aspiration — route infrastructure gaps to the data team and impact questions to evaluation-design-scoper.
